\ccomment{
\textbf{Comment AE.1.} I have received two referee reports, both recommending major revision.

The first referee is positive and finds the material publishable in Mathematical Programming after revision. Both referees view the main idea, using exact decision diagrams over chosen local supports as separation oracles for binary polynomial optimization, as useful and general.

The second referee, however, raises substantial concerns. Several contributions are adaptations of known machinery, and some claims are overstated, in particular the new polynomially solvable class and the facet-defining guarantee. Most importantly, the computational comparison omits the strongest available separation methods. This referee also asks for a more accessible presentation.
}

\ccomment{
\textbf{Comment AE.3 (Overall assessment).} The paper offers a useful and general tool, and its LABS results suggest a genuine advantage on dense instances where known inequality families struggle. However, it presents an adaptation of known machinery as more novel than it is, overstates a theoretical result, and builds its strongest computational evidence on a benchmark already solved at the root by exact RI separation, while omitting the exact separators available for RI and simple odd $\beta$-cycle inequalities, the presentation is dense and readers unfamiliar with DD-based cutting planes would benefit from more explanation.

I recommend major revision. If, after the requested comparisons, the DD cuts clearly outperform exact running intersection inequalities and simple odd $\beta$-cycle separation on LABS and other dense families, in gap closed per unit of time, the paper would merit publication. If they do not, the remaining contributions (the compact encoding, the width analysis and the facet audit) would in my view not suffice for a top-tier journal.
}

\ccomment{
\textbf{Comment R2.0 (Summary and strengths).} The paper uses exact decision diagrams (DDs), built on local sub-hypergraphs, as separation oracles for the multilinear polytope $MP_G$ of binary polynomial optimization (BPO). The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item a compact DD encoding that branches only on vertex variables and recovers hyperedge variables from the states, which also yields an extended formulation of $MP_G$;
\item an adaptation of the target-cut CGLP of Tjandraatmadja \& van Hoeve [32] to this encoding;
\item a characterization of DD width through ``valid antichains'', with bounded-width results for flowers and cycles and a claimed new polynomially solvable class;
\item a DD-based algorithm computing the dimension of the face induced by a valid inequality, used to audit the facetness of the cuts;
\item three support-selection strategies (fixed, expanded, partition), evaluated in a cut-and-branch scheme on the LABS and VISION benchmarks.
\end{itemize}
Strengths
\begin{itemize}
\item The compact encoding is natural and well suited to BPO.
\item Corollary 1 ($\mathrm{VAW}(G)=2^{\mathrm{pw}(G)}$ for graphs) is a clean link between the DD width and a classical parameter.
\item The dimension certificate (Algorithm 3) is simple, correct, and applicable beyond BPO.
\item Viewing a DD as a ``black-box separator over a chosen support'' is a good idea. The fixed-support experiment is a sensible sanity check.
\item The LABS results look promising (see major comment 3), on instances known to be hard for existing families of inequalities.
\end{itemize}
}

\ccomment{
\textbf{Comment R2.2 Theoretical claims.}\\
\textbf{Comment R2.2(a) (Theorem 1 and the ``new polynomially solvable class'').}
\begin{itemize}
\item Theorem 1 follows directly from its assumptions: if an order of polynomial width exists, the DD has polynomial size. It reads more like a technical lemma than a theorem, and it does not by itself exhibit a new class.
\item Polynomial solvability also requires that such an order can be found in polynomial time, which is not discussed.
\item The hypergraph used in Proposition 3 to show that bounded VAW does not imply bounded incidence treewidth, $E=\{A\cup\{b\}: b\in B\}$, lies in a well-understood class, since it is $\beta$-acyclic (it is even gamma-acyclic in fact).
\item Similarly, the first family in Proposition 3 (complete binary trees) is Berge-acyclic, and for such hypergraphs the standard linearization is already integral [18].
\item The authors should therefore exhibit a natural family with bounded VAW that is not covered by known tractable classes, such as $\beta$-acyclic hypergraphs or hypergraphs of bounded incidence treewidth [9]. Otherwise the claim of a ``new polynomially solvable class'' should be removed from the abstract, the introduction and the conclusions.
\end{itemize}
}

\ccomment{
\textbf{Comment R2.8(c) (The transition function).} The definition of $\phi_i$ is correct but hard to parse, and its verbal description does not match the formula.
\begin{itemize}

\item I suggest writing the transition explicitly as
\[
\phi_i(s_{i-1},z_{v_i})=\{e\in\mathcal A_i : e\cap U_{i-1}=\emptyset,\ z_{v_i}=1\}\ \cup\ \{e\in s_{i-1}\cap\mathcal A_i : v_i\notin e \text{ or } z_{v_i}=1\}.
\]
\end{itemize}
